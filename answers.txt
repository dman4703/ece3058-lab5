Problem 1B
----------

With 1 CPU the simulation ran for 67.6 s. Using 2 CPUs it ran for 36.2 s. 
With 4 CPUs, it was 33.6 s. Increasing the num of CPUs decreased the total execution time,
but this reduction was not linear. This is because not all work can be done in parallel, and
because there is extra overhead as more CPUs are added (such as context switches). Thus,
the execution time can not linearly decrease.

Problem 2B
----------

For timeslices of 800ms, 600ms, 400ms, and 200ms, the time in READY was 328.5 s, 313.9 s, 300.0 s, and 283.4 s respectively. 
This shows that the total waiting time decreases with shorter timeslices. However, shorter timeslices also increases the number
of context switches, creating more overhead. Thus, in a real OS, it is more optimal to choose a moderate timeslice to balance
overhead and responsiveness.
